# Prosumer / developer / small-fleet OBD-II telematics hardware with an open data path for fuel level on a 2021 Ford F-250 (gas) and 2021 Ford Expedition (US, as of 2026-10-25)

Scope note: this covers owner-purchased hardware that plugs into the OBD-II port and exposes fuel level over an HTTP-pollable API (vendor cloud or self-hosted). Smartcar/OEM-cloud paths are covered by other researchers. Labels used throughout: **[confirmed]** = read directly from a primary source; **[inferred]** = reasoned from confirmed facts; **[unverified]** = only secondary/retail/undated sources, or no source found.

## Key Question 0: Vendor fact table (summary; details and citations in the per-question sections below)

### Takeaway
Only two paths have documented, model-specific evidence of reading fuel level on a 2021 F-250 / 2021 Expedition over the OBD port without a fleet contract: (1) **Teltonika FMM003/FMC003-family OBD trackers**, whose official supported-vehicle list explicitly marks "F250 Super Duty 2008–2022 (USA)" and "Expedition 2018–2021 (USA)" for OEM fuel level in liters (plus a newer "Expedition 2018–" entry for fuel %), fed into self-hosted Traccar or hosted flespi; and (2) **DIMO's LTE R1** feeding the DIMO Telemetry API (`powertrainFuelSystemRelativeLevel`), on a free Hobbyist developer tier, though DIMO publishes no Ford-model-level fuel coverage and its retail price/longevity are murky. Geotab GO9 is technically strong on Ford but is sold only through resellers on quoted fleet plans; Samsara/Motive/Azuga are 36-month fleet contracts; Munic does not sell to individuals in any documented way; AutoPi/Freematics/WiCAN/Macchina are DIY platforms where fuel depends on you implementing the Ford-specific PID yourself.

### Inferences
- For a 2-vehicle private owner wanting an HTTP-pollable fuel %, the two realistic hardware routes are Teltonika FMM003 (+ 1NCE SIM + Traccar/flespi) and DIMO LTE R1 (+ Hobbyist API). Everything else is either fleet-contract-gated (Geotab, Samsara, Motive, Azuga), not sold to individuals (Munic), or DIY where Ford-specific fuel decoding is unsolved out of the box (AutoPi, Freematics, WiCAN, Macchina).
- The Teltonika F-250 row reports fuel in **liters** (OEM PID), not %, so the FordFuelAlert threshold logic would need tank-capacity conversion (2021 F-250 tank size not in scope here) or use the standard-PID AVL 48 % value if the truck answers PID 0x2F (which Q5 evidence suggests it may not).

### Gaps
- No vendor publishes a per-model "does the 2021 F-250 7.3L/6.2L report fuel level" statement except Teltonika's list.
- Geotab, Samsara, Motive: no documented path for a non-business single-vehicle account.

## Key Question 1: DIMO in 2026 — usable by an individual to read own car's fuel? Cost of LTE R1/Macaron; gas Ford fuel; crypto required?; Smartcar-free; reliability after restructuring

### Takeaway
Yes on paper: DIMO's developer platform has a free "Hobbyist" tier explicitly aimed at "enthusiasts building for their own vehicles" ($1.25/vehicle/mo), the Telemetry API (GraphQL) defines `powertrainFuelSystemRelativeLevel` (0–100 %), and the hardware route (LTE R1) is Smartcar-free. The crypto requirement appears to have been removed for licensing (docs now say licenses are FREE and consumer subscriptions are card-only), but older docs still describe $DIMO/MATIC wallets — treat as transitional. DIMO does not publish Ford-model-level fuel-signal coverage, the R1 retail price could not be confirmed from the official store (rate-limited), and connectivity after the included 2 years is explicitly "no decision has been made."

- Telemetry API queries need a Vehicle JWT obtained via a Developer License from the Developer Console; Vehicle JWTs expire after 10 minutes — [DIMO docs](https://docs.dimo.org/developer-platform/api-references/telemetry-api/nodes-and-objects/signalslatest) **[confirmed via snippet]**
- Energy signals offered: "Battery state of charge, Charging status, Range, Fuel level"; "Availability varies by make and connection type"; data "delivered over GraphQL" via a single endpoint; SDKs for TypeScript, Python, C# — [dimo.org/vehicle-data-api](https://dimo.org/vehicle-data-api) **[confirmed]**

### Inferences
- Total DIMO cost for 2 gas Fords, year 1 (after any included trial): 2 × R1 hardware (unknown, est. $100–$300 each) + 2 × $89.99/yr Pro + 2 × $1.25/mo Hobbyist vehicle fee ≈ $210/yr recurring + hardware. Connectivity after year 2 is an open liability.
- The R1 is an OBD dongle; whether it populates `powertrainFuelSystemRelativeLevel` on a 2021 F-250/Expedition depends on DIMO's firmware implementing Ford's non-standard fuel PID (see Q5). DIMO's "Availability varies by make" plus the Mondeo/F-150 evidence that standard PID 0x2F is absent on Fords means this must be tested, not assumed.
- Smartcar-free: yes — the R1 path is pure hardware + DIMO cloud. No crypto wallet appears necessary for an individual in 2026 (card-only subscriptions, free license), but the token economy still exists underneath (rewards paid in $DIMO).

## Key Question 5: Technical ground truth — does a 2021 F-250 / Expedition answer Mode 01 PID 0x2F? If not, which Ford PID carries fuel level, and which devices implement it?

### Takeaway
Evidence points to **no standard PID 0x2F** on modern Fords: a 2020 F-150 owner reported PID 2F "wasn't returning anything", and a Ford Mondeo owner got "no data" for standard fuel and found only Mode 22 DID **0xF42F** (request `22F42F`, response from 0x7E8, last byte ≈ percent) tracked the gauge. Ford extended-PID lists for Torque give `22F42F` (A×0.39216 %) for the 6.7L Super Duty and `2216C1` for older trucks. On the CAN bus, comma.ai's Ford DBC defines `FuelLvl_Pc_Dsply` (10-bit, factor 0.108696, offset −5.217) in message 0x434 `Cluster_Info_3_FD1` and `FuelLvl_Pc_DsplyEng` in 0x424 `Powertrain_Data_4` flagged for MY2021 P702 (F-150). No source was found that tests these on a 2021 F-250 or Expedition specifically.

### Cited Findings
- "listed as 2F, but it wasn't returning anything" — 2020 F-150 owner, standard fuel-level PID — [Ford Truck Enthusiasts: Fuel Level PID](https://www.ford-trucks.com/forums/1674086-fuel-level-pid.html) **[confirmed via search snippet; page returned 403 on fetch, replies not read]**
- Ford Mondeo Mk4 (RealDash, Jan 2024): standard fuel PID "just isn't sent"; Mode 22 candidates "22f42f or 22161"; "22F42F … the only pid to have shown a change in value which matches the fuel level change"; replies from "7E8"; readings 0x13 (low), 0x09 (empty light), 0x67 (=103 decimal, right after refuel) — [RealDash forum](https://forum.realdash.net/t/obd2-ford-fuel-level-code/4568) **[confirmed]**
- Torque extended-PID list for Ford 6.7L (Super Duty diesel): "Fuel Tank Level – PID – 22f42f – Done Testing", scaling A×0.39216 % — [torque-bhp.com](https://torque-bhp.com/community/main-forum/ford-6-7l-extended-pid-list/) **[confirmed via snippet; page 503 on fetch]**
- Older Ford Torque list: "FUEL LEVEL FUEL PID: 2216C1 % Max/Min: 100/0" — [Ford Truck Enthusiasts: Torque App Ford Extended PIDs](https://www.ford-trucks.com/forums/1073405-torque-app-ford-extended-pids.html) **[confirmed via snippet; older trucks]**
- Model-year drift: a 2021 F-150 owner reports PIDs that worked on a 2011 truck "no longer works on my 2021" — [f150gen14.com Torque Pro custom PIDs](https://www.f150gen14.com/forum/threads/torque-pro-custom-pids.14061/) **[confirmed via snippet]**
- FORScan staff: "FORScan has no virtual PIDs, all parameters are read directly from ECUs"; "uses service protocol, not OBDII protocol (although OBDII protocol is supported too)"; a parameter is readable "IF the PID is supported by module" — [FORScan forum, Nov 2015](https://forum.forscan.org/viewtopic.php?p=6974) **[confirmed; dated 2015]**
- FORScan IPC configuration has a "Fuel Level Source" parameter that must stay set to PCM; on 2023+ P708 F-Series tank size lives only in the PCM — [FORScan forum: adjust fuel tank size and DTE](https://forum.forscan.org/viewtopic.php?p=105685) **[confirmed via snippet]** → fuel level originates in the PCM and is broadcast to the IPC **[inferred]**
- CAN DBC (comma.ai opendbc, `ford_lincoln_base_pt.dbc`, fetched 2026-10-25): `BO_ 1076 Cluster_Info_3_FD1: 8 GWM` contains `SG_ FuelLvl_Pc_Dsply : 1|10@0+ (0.108696,-5.217408) [-5.217408|105.9786] "Percent"  VDM,ECM_Diesel`, plus raw sender values `FuelLvlPssvSide_No_Raw`/`FuelLvlActvSide_No_Raw` (10-bit, 0–1023) and `FuelLvlWarn_D_Actl`; `BO_ 1060 Powertrain_Data_4: 8 PCM_HEV` contains `SG_ FuelLvl_Pc_DsplyEng : 41|10@0+ (0.108696,-5.217408) … "percent"  GWM` with attributes `U_P702_MY2021_Tx` and `U_T6_MCA_MY2020_Tx` — [opendbc raw DBC](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/commaai/opendbc/master/opendbc/dbc/ford_lincoln_base_pt.dbc) **[confirmed — grepped locally]** (1076 = 0x434; 1060 = 0x424; P702 is the 2021+ F-150 platform code **[inferred from attribute naming]**)
- Devices implementing Ford-specific fuel: Teltonika OEM profile (AVL 390, "vehicle specific PID") for F250 Super Duty/Expedition — see Q4; Geotab GO lists `fuel_lvl` % and Ford "Fuel level input" — see Q3; DIMO exposes `powertrainFuelSystemRelativeLevel` with make-dependent availability — see Q1; WiCAN CAN-to-JSON lets a user decode a raw frame with a math expression — [Crowd Supply WiCAN update](https://www.crowdsupply.com/meatpi-electronics/wican/updates/new-feature-and-home-assistant-example) **[confirmed]**
- AutoPi/Freematics are generic: "some cars doesn't fully implement the OBD2 standard" (AutoPi support on empty fuel level) — [AutoPi community](https://community.autopi.io/t/missing-data-information-in-dashboard/365) **[confirmed via snippet]**; Freematics "reads information from your car, so it depends if your car reports fuel info" — [Traccar forum](https://traccar.org/forums/topic/does-freematics-one-model-h-have-a-fuel-sensor) **[confirmed]**

### Inferences
- A 2021 F-250 (gas) and 2021 Expedition very likely do **not** answer Mode 01 PID 0x2F (consistent 2020-F-150 and Mondeo reports; Ford relies on UDS DIDs). The practical device-side options are: (a) UDS request `22 F4 2F` to the PCM (0x7E0 → 0x7E8), scale A×0.392 %; (b) passively read CAN 0x434 `FuelLvl_Pc_Dsply` (or 0x424 on 2021 P702) on HS-CAN with factor 0.108696 and offset −5.2174. Both are "Ford manufacturer-specific" paths; any dongle without a Ford profile will show fuel level blank.
- Teltonika's liter-valued OEM fuel implies its profile uses a different DID (volume rather than %), or converts; the newer Expedition 2018– profile's % column suggests they added a percent DID in 2025.
- Any DIY device (AutoPi, Freematics, WiCAN, Macchina) can be programmed with `22F42F` or the 0x434 decode, but validation on each truck is required (the Mondeo reading exceeded 100 %, so raw scaling isn't universal).

### Gaps
- No forum post or vendor note found that tests fuel-level PIDs on a 2021 F-250 7.3L/6.2L or 2021 Expedition specifically; closest are 2020/2021 F-150 and 6.7L Super Duty diesel.
- Could not read the ford-trucks.com thread replies (HTTP 403) to confirm whether `22F42F` was validated on the 2020 F-150.
- The DBC does not say which CAN bus (HS1/HS3) carries 0x434 on Super Duty/Expedition (U625/P558 platforms), nor whether 0x424 `FuelLvl_Pc_DsplyEng` exists on non-P702 trucks.

## Key Question 6: Safety / caveats of a permanently installed OBD dongle on a 2021 Ford (battery drain, BCM/alarm interactions, dealer/warranty posture)

### Takeaway
Ford's owner's manual (F-150 edition dated 2018/08, same wording quoted by Maverick owners for MY2022) explicitly says the DLC is for diagnostic tools, that aftermarket devices using it during normal driving "may cause interference with or even damage to vehicle systems", that Ford "does not recommend or endorse" plug-in devices unless Ford-approved, and that the warranty "will not cover damage caused by an aftermarket plug-in device." Technically, the OBD port is always powered and a technician on an F-150 Lightning forum notes that polling modules over OBD keeps them awake; real-world dead-battery cases from dongles exist (Toyota/Motor1). Mitigation is a device with proper sleep (Teltonika/Geotab-class) or one that stops polling when voltage drops.

### Cited Findings
- Ford manual text: "Your vehicle has an OBD Data Link Connector (DLC) that is used in conjunction with a diagnostic scan tool for vehicle diagnostics, repairs and reprogramming services. Installing an aftermarket device that uses the DLC during normal driving for purposes such as remote insurance company monitoring... may cause interference with or even damage to vehicle systems"; Ford "does not recommend or endorse" plug-in devices unless approved; "Warranty will not cover damage caused by an aftermarket plug-in device" — [2019 F-150 Raptor Owner's Manual PDF](https://autooutlet.cz/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/19-f150-raptor-manual-en.pdf) **[confirmed via snippet; edition 201808]**; same language discussed for the 2022 Maverick manual — [Maverick Truck Club thread](https://www.mavericktruckclub.com/forum/threads/maverick-owners-warned-not-to-use-obd-11-connector.39292/post-705013) **[confirmed via snippet]**
- Anecdote: a Maverick owner's dealer refused a warranty diagnostic on a dead OBD port, blaming aftermarket LED lighting wired to the circuit — [Maverick Truck Club](https://www.mavericktruckclub.com/forum/threads/maverick-owners-warned-not-to-use-obd-11-connector.39292/post-704051) **[single anecdote]**
- Module wake: "polling the modules via OBD will keep them awake and/or wake them up even though the truck is otherwise off" (technician); another owner monitors the dongle's 12 V supply so it stops making requests once the truck is off — [F-150 Lightning Forum](https://www.f150lightningforum.com/forum/goto/post?id=610188) **[confirmed via snippet]**
